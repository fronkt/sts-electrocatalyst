## S29420 — GDY@RuO2

**Identity and source pins.** DOI 10.26599/nr.2026.94908680. Main: `results/s2_2026-09-25/full_text/download_si_review_2026-10-03/files/S29420/S29420_main.pdf`, SHA-256 `a61266461ec532e28c31989557b729bb336123dac48a7fb11ff5d12fc91d7dbf`; transcription `.../files/S29420/S29420_main_reading.txt`. SI: `.../files/S29420/8680_ESM.pdf`, SHA-256 `0747fe7af51e9b2cb09478eaf7320dd04a78c2a837b25f13886506de4e747be8`; transcription `.../files/S29420/S29420_si_reading.txt`. Source gate records identity and SI completeness as verified.

**Model and surface.** SI PDF p. 2, “Calculation methods,” says: “construct a RuO2 (110) and GDY (002) surface model.” The same section specifies five O–Ru–O layers and 144 atoms for RuO2(110), then five layers plus one GDY layer and 333 atoms for GDY@RuO2. During electrocatalytic simulation the slab lattice is fixed under compressive strain. Main PDF p. 4, §3.1, describes pristine RuO2 and GDY@RuO2 as the DFT models. The comparison is therefore an ideal rutile RuO2(110)/GDY(002) interface model, not a model whose composition or active-site census is enumerated in the methods.

**Reported energetics and competing pathway.** Main PDF p. 4, §3.1, transcription lines 375–403, states that “Gibbs free energy calculations of both adsorbate evolution mechanism (AEM) and LOM pathways” were performed on pristine RuO2 and GDY@RuO2. The authors say bare RuO2 prefers LOM and report its LOM overpotential as 0.5 eV at 1.23 V. For AEM, they give *OH formation 0.59 vs 1.01 eV, *OH dehydrogenation 1.22 vs 1.01 eV, and *O-to-*OOH 1.79 vs 1.96 eV for GDY@RuO2 vs RuO2, then explicitly call the resulting quantity “the theoretical AEM overpotential (η) at U = 1.23 V.” They report 0.59 eV for GDY@RuO2 and 0.75 eV for RuO2−x. Preserve that direct author-reported pair and the reported unit eV.

Main PDF p. 4, §3.1, lines 379–388, supplies the competing-mechanism explanation: bare RuO2 prefers LOM; ΔG(*OOH−*O) is 2.47 eV on RuO2 and rises to 2.98 eV on GDY@RuO2; the authors interpret that increase as suppressing LOM and favoring AEM. The main text says acid AEM comprises four steps (p. 4, lines 389–400), but does not enumerate the proton/electron half-reactions there.

**Potential reference and calculation details.** The main text defines U=1.23 V as “the theoretical potential required for driving the OER process in theoretical calculations”; it does not label this computational U as vs RHE. The calculation-methods SI (PDF p. 2) reports VASP/PBE, PAW, 450 eV plane-wave cutoff, Gaussian smearing width 0.05 eV, self-consistency threshold 10−5 eV, force threshold 0.05 eV/Å, DFT-D3 dispersion, 20 Å vacuum, and 1×1×1 surface-structure k-point sampling. It gives `G = Eads + ZPE − TS`. I found no explicit CHE equation/reference scale or one-electron reaction-step equations in the main/SI text transcriptions. The experimental paragraph converting measured potentials to RHE is not evidence for the computational reference and is not transferred here.

**Retained discrepancy.** SI PDF p. 4, Fig. S5 caption, independently says the AEM “overpotential was lowered from 0.73 eV of RuO2 to 0.56 eV (GDY@RuO2).” Thus the source contains a direct main/SI discrepancy: main 0.59/0.75 eV versus SI 0.56/0.73 eV. Keep both pairs on record; no averaging, recomputation, or correction is supported by the sources.

**Model and site set.** Main PDF p. 7, §3.3, transcription lines 531–537: “20 Ru1−xIrxO2(110) slabs” with Ru/Ir randomly placed among 24 metal lattice sites; each slab has three top-surface cus sites. Main p. 7, lines 565–572, describes 62 cus sites across the local arrangements on these slabs and similar pure RuO2(110)/IrO2(110) slabs. Table S3 lists per-site composition `x`, site element and surface number. The measured activity optimum at Ru0.5Ir0.5O2 is a bulk experimental composition and does not collapse the DFT model set to that single composition.

**Author-assigned limiting-potential quantity.** Main PDF p. 7, §3.3, lines 569–572 says Table S3 reports the site-specific maximum among ΔG1–ΔG4 at 0 V vs RHE and explicitly states this “corresponds to the limiting potential of that site.” Main PDF p. 8–9, §3.3, lines 602–605, describes limiting potential as the free energy needed to drive the least facile reaction step spontaneously. This explicit assignment is the source-grounded per-site limiting-potential equivalent. The tabulated quantity is reported as a free-energy maximum in eV at U=0 V vs RHE; retain that unit and author label. Do not calculate η by subtracting 1.23 V, substitute Figure 10 activity fractions, or use the separate 1.5 V activity statement as η.

**CHE steps, intermediates, and alternatives.** SI DOCX paragraphs 108–110 enumerate the cus-site states: vacant `*`, `*OH`, `O*+Hb`, `O*`, `*OOH`, and `O2*+Hb`, with gas-phase H2 and H2O reference energies. Paragraph 110 explicitly gives CHE as `G(H+ + e−) = 1/2 G(H2(g)) − eU`, where U is vs RHE, and says the four OER steps are in Table S2. SI paragraphs 145–158 define the four one-electron steps: H2O+* → *OH (or O*+Hb)+H++e−; *OH → O*+H++e−; O* → *OOH (or O2*+Hb)+H++e−; and *OOH → *+O2+H++e−. Table S3 caption/paragraph 163 lists the adsorption free energies and the maximum of ΔG1–ΔG4 at U=0 V vs RHE.

SI paragraph 109 reports zero-point/entropy corrections of 0.35 eV for *OH and O*+Hb formation, 0.05 eV for O* formation, and 0.40 eV for *OOH and O2*+Hb formation. Main PDF p. 7, lines 541–553, cautions that this CHE modeling yields thermodynamic spontaneity, not activation energies. A second pathway restricts intermediates to cus positions, excluding O*+Hb and O2*+Hb (main p. 7, lines 563–565); the bridge-enabled and cus-only cases yield different predicted activity behavior (main p. 10, lines 681–704).

**DFT settings and page split.** Main PDF §2.6 spans pp. 4–5. In the transcription, the page-5 marker begins at line 385; most settings are before that marker on p. 4. Lines 363–384 specify GPAW with ASE, 500 eV plane-wave cutoff, RPBE, spin-paired calculations, rutile (110) 3×1 slab, 2×2 k-point sampling, four atomic layers with bottom two fixed, and 20 Å vacuum. The text continues on p. 5, lines 386–389, with dipole correction, relaxation to forces below 0.05 eV Å−1, and use of CHE. The main p. 7 pathway discussion says neighboring cus sites were capped with O*; bridge-coupled alternatives involve Hb, hydrogen on neighboring bridge oxygen.

**Mechanistic uncertainty.** Main PDF p. 7–8, lines 581–600, says Ru cus starts prefer O*+Hb over *OH, while Ir cus starts stabilize *OH over O*+Hb; O2*+Hb is preferred over *OOH for both cus occupations. Main p. 7 says CHE cannot calculate activation energies. The direct limiting-potential assignment is clear, while pathway choice and predicted surface activity depend on whether bridge-bound intermediates are admitted. The highlighted most-active sites in SI are a Table S3 presentation feature; they are not the basis for replacing the author’s p. 7 limiting-potential assignment.
