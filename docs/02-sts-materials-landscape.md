# 02 — STS Materials Landscape (what actually places)

Evidence-grounded analysis of Regeneron STS, 2021–2026. Materials is a
consistent **minority** track: roughly **2–4 of the Top 40 Finalists** and
**~15–25 of the Top 300 Scholars** each year, out-punched at the top by
biomedical, comp-bio, math, and physics/astro.

## Thermal management — specifically

**No Top-40 finalist 2021–2026 was a classic high-κ / heat-dissipation project.**
The thermal-adjacent placements (Low, Zhang, Kim, and Corey's thermal *imaging*)
all reached Scholar or used thermal as a method, not the deliverable. → white
space + a Scholar ceiling unless elevated with method novelty + a sharp
application hook.

## Computational / ML — honest read

- **Computational DOES reach Top 40** (Guan, Iyer, Andreasen, Evan Kim,
  D'Halleweyn) — not disqualifying.
- **But the modern top skews experimental/hybrid** (2025–26 finalists all have
  synthesis, characterization, or device testing).
- **The winning computational pattern is method novelty** — a new
  generative/inverse-design framework that *proposes and ideally validates*
  candidates, benchmarked against published baselines. "Inverse design /
  generative discovery" is the recurring winning template.

## Five takeaways

1. Application-anchored lanes (batteries, catalysis, solar) are the safest
   high-ceiling bets. Thermal/RE need an extra novelty + "so what" to break Scholar.
2. **Go hybrid:** ML/DFT designs or screens + ≥1 experimentally synthesized-and-
   characterized candidate.
3. If purely computational, **make the method the novelty** and benchmark it.
4. Secure real lab/mentor access early; finalist materials work nearly always
   rides on university/national-lab instruments.
5. Engineer **one crisp, quantified contribution** and a generalist-legible "so
   what"; over-invest in the finalist interview / independence story.
