# 25 — STS 2027 application playbook: formatting, submission, judging, and how scholars/finalists are made

Deep research on the Regeneron STS application itself (2026-07-01 web research; sources at
the end). Primary sources: the official **STS 2026** Research Report Guidelines, Application
Questions preview, and Society for Science FAQ/judging pages (the 2027 cycle reuses the same
structure; 2027-specific dates confirmed on the FAQ). This is the compliance + strategy
reference for the Cu-Fe(-X) conductor entry ([docs/24](24-thermal-pivot-execution-plan.md)).

Money: every scholar gets **$2,000** (+ **$2,000 to the school** per scholar); every finalist
gets **≥ $25,000**; top 10 range **$40,000 → $250,000** (1st). Total pool ~$1.8M. STS 2026
had **~2,600 entrants → 300 scholars (~11.5%) → 40 finalists (~1.5%)**.

Announcements are made simultaneously by press release (societyforscience.org + Regeneron
investor page + GlobeNewswire) and on the Society's scholars/finalists list pages; local
press picks up from there. There is no portal login reveal — the lists are public.

## 2. What you actually submit (the 11 tasks)

From the official Application Questions preview (2026 edition):

1. **Task 1 — Basic info**: eligibility attestations, family-in-STEM disclosure (150 w — any
   relative in STEM/academia **must** be listed; failure to disclose = failure to qualify).
2. **Tasks 2a–c — Recommendations** (all due Nov 5, confidential):
   - **Educator recommendation** (up to 2) — person who knows you best academically; can be a
     non-science teacher; second slot can be a coach/advisor.
   - **Project recommendation** (up to 2) — the person *closest to the research*. A lab head
     should only write it if they actually worked with you.
   - **High School Report** — counselor uploads official transcript as PDF + school profile.
3. **Task 3 — Rules Wizard**: branching compliance questionnaire (timeline of the project,
   data provenance, then human/animal/tissue/PHBA/**hazardous materials & activities**
   sections with uploads). See §6.
4. **Task 4 — Science Research Description**: category, title (AP Title Case), mentors, how
   the idea originated (250 w), duration (75 w), **"What Did You Do?" — six 200-word boxes**
   (purpose / design / implementation / gathering data / analysis / conclusions), limitations
   (200 w), **AI-use disclosure (100 w)**, published-paper questions, payment disclosures,
   conflict-of-interest disclosure (200 w), Statement of Independence (200 w).
5. **Task 5 — Research Report upload** (the 20-page paper; format spec in §3).
6. **Task 6 — Previous research**: prior individual projects, any past *team* research
   (team research is ineligible as the entry itself), conference talks/preprints, publications.
7. **Task 7 — Essays** (see §5).
8. **Task 8 — Activities/awards**: up to 7 activities incl. family responsibilities & jobs;
   last-3-summers question (200 w); awards (250 w); fun fact (75 w).
9. **Task 9 — Test scores (optional)**: SAT/ACT/AP/IB; superscoring permitted; omitting them
   is explicitly not held against you.
10. **Task 10 — Beyond the project**: contacts, mentor contact info, "most positive influence
    on your scientific endeavors" (they send that person a letter).
11. **Ethics Statement + Submit**: signature; certifications incl. **"I have not used AI
    tools, like ChatGPT, to construct the research report or responses to questions in the
    application."** Application must be *submitted* (green button), then download a copy
    before the deadline — you can't retrieve it afterward.

## 3. Research Report — the exact formatting spec

From the official Research Report Guidelines (repeat-offender items bolded):

- **≤ 20 pages** of content; no minimum; pages past 20 are simply not read.
  **Title page, abstract page, and bibliography do NOT count** toward the 20.
  **Appendices DO count** toward the 20.
- Page 1 = title page (name + project title required; lab/mentor names permitted; **no
  email/phone**). Page 2 = abstract. Bibliography at the end.
- Recommended (not mandated) body: short intro/background & purpose → experimental design
  (methods + results) → concluding discussion of results and implications. Discipline-standard
  ordering is fine.
- **Font must render ≥ Times New Roman 11 pt** (captions may be smaller if legible);
  **1.5 line spacing**; **1-inch margins all sides**; **single column only**; shrinking
  font/margins to gain words is grounds for disqualification.
- Page numbers bottom-right, starting **after the abstract**.
- **Every image, graph, table, chart — including ones you made yourself — must be cited**
  per the Citation Guide (Appendix 3 of the Official Rules). A missing/incorrect figure
  citation can disqualify the entry. (This is the most mechanical DQ trap in the whole app.)
- Internal citations + bibliography covering *all* sources consulted.
- **No hyperlinks anywhere** except in bibliography entries — evaluators are forbidden from
  clicking links, so nothing may depend on external material.
- No photos of people unless scientifically necessary (with consent).
- First person "I" is acceptable where the field would write "we" — and is *encouraged*
  framing for clarifying what you did independently; otherwise passive voice.
- Don't pad: no literature-review chapters, no lengthy history of prior work, no
  autobiographical material.
- File: **PDF ≤ 4 MB** (system may allow 5; keep to 4), named
  **`LASTNAME.FIRSTNAME.ZIPCODE`**, not a scanned/photographed image PDF. After uploading,
  re-download the application PDF to check symbols survived.
- Published work may be submitted only if formatting complies; not recommended unless
  sole/first author — otherwise write an STS-specific version highlighting your own
  contribution and acknowledge the published paper.
- Adults may *review and suggest*; they may **not rewrite or supply replacement text**.

Ineligible outright: proposals, incomplete investigations ("results to date" of an unfinished
study), literature reviews, essays, team projects. **You must have completed results by the
deadline** — no post-deadline supplements.

## 4. AI policy — the single most important compliance item for this project

Two distinct regimes, and the distinction is load-bearing for us:

1. **AI *inside* the research = permitted + must be disclosed.** Task 4 has an explicit
   "Did you use AI in this project or Research Report or application in any way?" (100 w)
   question; the FAQ says AI use in projects "is permitted, and should be disclosed" and
   cited. There is a **2027 AI Usage Chart** on the STS resource page — pull it when drafting
   the disclosure. Our NEP/HNEMD surrogates, qNEHVI BO loop, and any LLM-assisted *code*
   fall here: disclose, cite, done. There's even a "Research Report Similarities" question
   (150 w) to pre-explain anything an AI/plagiarism detector might flag.
2. **AI *writing* the report/essays/answers = prohibited and certified against.** The
   Research Report Guidelines open with "The Student Researcher is required to write the
   paper without the use of AI," and the Ethics Statement makes it a signed certification.
   Applications are screened for authenticity; violation = disqualification + possible ban
   from Society programs.

**Consequence for our workflow:** everything in this repo (Claude-drafted plans, analysis
notes, this doc) is fine as *infrastructure*, but the 20-page report, all essay answers, and
all application text must be drafted by Frank, in Frank's words, start to finish. Claude may
be used the way any adult reviewer may be used — pointing at weaknesses, checking compliance
against this doc — but may not generate or rewrite submission prose. Plan writing time
accordingly (see §8).

## 5. Essays and short answers — where scholars are actually separated

Word limits are maxima; concise is explicitly encouraged; first or third person OK.

| Prompt | Limit | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Layperson's summary | 200 w | Background + procedure + conclusion + relevance, readable by journalists/public. This is the abstract-for-humans; polish it hardest. |
| Benefits & impact | 200 w | "What benefit, and *what additional steps by whom* are needed to realize it" — they want a realistic deployment chain, not hype. |
| Potential as a scientist/engineer | 200 w | Concrete evidence of aptitude/leadership/initiative; other STEM interests; 10-year plan. This essay carries the "scientist search" weight. |
| Common App prompt (pick 1 of 3) | 350 w | They *prefer you think beyond the research project* here. |
| What Did You Do (6 boxes, Task 4) | 6 × 200 w | "The bulk of the application." Attribute every piece of help, then claim what's yours. Elaborate beyond the paper. |
| Idea origin | 250 w | The story behind the topic; "was it assigned?" |
| Limitations | 200 w | Honest constraint accounting (equipment, money, age, access). |
| Statement of Independence | 200 w | Disclose every influence, even loose ones (dinner-table exposure counts). Non-disclosure is the violation, not the influence. |

Payment disclosure: any paid program/coaching/mentor fees **must** be disclosed —
"entrants who do not disclose involvement in a paid program will be disqualified." All the
listed options are permitted; only concealment kills. (Paying for cloud compute/materials is
not on the list of program/coaching categories, but there's a catch-all "fees of some sort
I'd like to explain" option — cheap insurance to note self-funded compute/materials there.)

## 6. Rules Wizard implications for the Cu-Fe project

- **Timeline questions (all entrants)**: when did you start brainstorming / request
  approvals / begin data collection / conclude data collection; how was data obtained; data
  types; was it done at a Regulated Research Institution. **Our repo ledger (docs/, commit
  history) is the provenance record — keep it current; answers must reconcile.**
  Data collection must be *concluded* for whatever the report claims.
- **Hazardous materials & activities (Part VII)** will trigger for us: furnace melt (FWM),
  wire drawing, mechanical testing. Expect a risk assessment + designated supervision
  paperwork upload. Get the exact form from the Rules Wizard early (open a real application
  now — Task 1 has a harmless "testing the application" path is NOT for us; open the real
  one, fill nothing binding, and read Task 3's branches). Preapprovals are asked about
  *by date* — do the risk assessment **before** the melt campaign, not retroactively.
- No humans/vertebrates/tissue/PHBAs → those sections stay collapsed.
- **Individual project**: no other high-school student may touch the research, and it must
  stay individual through June 2027 (no combining into a team entry elsewhere).
- Mentorship is optional: "If you did not work with any mentors, you may skip or discuss
  your level of independence." Fully-independent is a valid and even advantaged posture *if
  documented* — but a Project Recommender "closest to the research" is still required, so
  cultivate at least one adult (teacher/professor contact) with real visibility into the
  work. Budget for this now; it cannot be conjured in October.

## 7. How judging works, and what separates entrant → scholar → finalist

Pipeline (from the Society's judging page):

1. Eligibility screen (deadline, qualification, originality/authenticity screening).
2. **Three or more PhD-level reviewers in the subject area** score the full entry —
   report, essays, recommendations — "with greatest weight given to the Research Report."
3. Top 300 → **Scholars** (~Jan 7).
4. Scholars re-reviewed by a **panel of 15 distinguished scientists across disciplines**
   → 40 **Finalists** (~Jan 21). Note the audience shift: stage-2 readers are in-field;
   the finalist panel is *cross-disciplinary* — the entry must land with a physicist AND a
   biologist.
5. Finals Week: **project judging** (defend the research) + **panel judging** (breadth:
   problem-solving across all of STEM, famously puzzle-style questions outside your field)
   + public exhibition → top 10.

Stated criteria: scientific merit/originality/rigor of the research; the student's
*individual contribution and independence*; academic aptitude incl. communication; and
future STEM leadership potential. Explicitly NOT criteria: perfection, specific test
scores, famous labs, state/school quotas. STS repeatedly brands itself a **scientist
search, not a science-project search** — the person is judged as much as the paper.

Practical reading of the scholar/finalist bar (consistent with our precedent survey in
[docs/18](18-competitive-benchmark.md)):

- **Scholar**: complete, original, rigorous individual research + a clean, honest,
  well-written application. Many scholars have "modest" projects executed and communicated
  excellently.
- **Finalist**: research sophistication approaching grad-level *plus* unmistakable
  ownership *plus* an application that reads as a future PI — leadership, breadth,
  articulate impact story. The cross-disciplinary panel at stage 4 rewards clarity and
  significance over technical density.

## 8. Tips & tricks from past finalists/scholars (distilled)

Named advice from the Society's alumni-advice post (finalists incl. Kevin Shen '25 7th
place, Charlotte Rosario, Minghao Zou, Siddharth Nirgudkar, Chloe Lee, Elisa Zhang,
Angeline Zhao, et al.):

- **Tell it as a story** — the struggle, the dead ends, the day it worked. Judges read
  thousands of pages; narrative memory beats spec-sheet memory.
- **Personality in essays** — curiosity and enthusiasm are scored signals, not fluff.
  Don't re-summarize the paper in the essays; reveal the person who did it.
- **Why > what**: significance and impact framing ("what would have to happen, and by
  whom, for this to matter") is a dedicated essay for a reason.
- **Document as you go** — the Rules Wizard timeline questions and the six What-Did-You-Do
  boxes are trivial if you kept a ledger, and painful archaeology if not. (Our repo
  discipline already does this; keep dating things.)
- **Start the application early, writing takes longer than expected** — Chloe Lee's
  specific warning. Essays into Word docs, not composed in the portal.
- **Brief your recommenders**: resume, essay drafts, project timeline, what STS is looking
  for; request in September, not late October. Recommendations arriving late = application
  incomplete, no exceptions.
- **Submit even if unsure** — repeated finalist theme ("weren't sure they should apply,
  always glad they did"). ~11.5% to scholar is not a lottery for a real project.
- **For finals prep** (if it comes to that): practice explaining the work to non-experts;
  expect cross-disciplinary puzzle questions probing how you *think*, not what you
  memorized; know the field context around the project, not just the project.

Common killers, per coaching-guide consensus: unclear personal contribution; claims that
outrun the data; generic essays; recommenders with no real visibility; last-minute rush;
and (mechanical) figure-citation / font / page-limit violations.
