## 1. VERDICT ON THE STS QUESTION

**Scholar: yes, comfortably — probably top-100 of the 300.** The first cut is three in-field PhDs weighting the Research Report most heavily. This project has a dated pre-registration with six amendments, a threshold that fired and forced a real withdrawal, six closed negatives measured rather than argued, a Hessian that mechanically confirms a saddle point, and an exact lemma verified to 1e-9. That is above the finalist median on rigor. Being purely computational is not a barrier — six of the 2026 top ten had no wet lab and 2025's first place was a single-author computational paper (Lens 6, confirmed from program books; STS sources carry no DOI and are marked UNVERIFIED by convention).

**Finalist: no, not as it stands today.** Four specific reasons, in order of force:

1. **There is no artifact.** The deliverable today is a withdrawal plus a three-item taxonomy. Every methods-genre finalist title on the 2023 list names an object — "a technique," "a network," "bounds." Lens 6 found no finalist in five years whose headline was a diagnosis with no remedy. That claim is its own least-confident finding (~60–70 of ~200 projects in the window, 2025/2026 title indexes never retrieved), so treat it as directional, not absolute: constructive artifacts are the overwhelming norm and the burden is on this project to justify the exception. It currently has no justification.

2. **The second cut is 15 cross-disciplinary judges scoring "scientific rigor *and* world-changing potential."** "Three silent error classes in DFT screening" is at ceiling on the first and near floor on the second. The fix is not spin; it is that something must change as a result of the work.

3. **Two of the most quotable results have live, unread prior art.** Briquet 2017 (10.1002/cctc.201601662) already published that setup choice moves the rutile-(110) OER descriptor by >0.6 eV — and its actual title is *"A New Type of Scaling Relations to Assess the Accuracy of Computational Predictions..."*, i.e. it is itself a scaling-relations-as-error-diagnostic paper, which is closer prior art on the *framing* of Finding 5 than the sweep acknowledged. Chatterjee et al. (arXiv:2512.05938, Dec 2025, abstract verified) already computed overpotentials across all of OC20/OC22 at σ ≈ 0.3–0.5 eV and concluded the uncertainty misclassifies a broad fraction as good. Neither has been read in full. Finding 4 as currently worded collides with the second; the novelty framing collides with the first.

4. **Registration discipline is currently outrunning data-reading, and that is dangerous for this project specifically.** ANATOMY's P-SPLIT registers "U moves c_M by less than 0.10 eV on ≥5 of 7 metals," with a falsifier at >0.20 eV. **6. The 2×1o cell arm — DROP.** Block 1A closed ADOPT_2X1V. A third cell adds a row nobody asked for, cannot be crossed with anything in the remaining time, and creates a two-protocols-in-one-table hazard. Cite the closed 1A result.

**7. BEEF-vdW η error bars (Stage E) — RUN MODIFIED, gated on the S0 pre-flight.** Protocol in S5.
