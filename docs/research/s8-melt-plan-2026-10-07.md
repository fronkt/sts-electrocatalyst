## Batch 1 — what it is and where it comes from

Batch 1 is the **MLIP set**: the six alloys whose activity a machine-learned interatomic potential (MACE-MPA-0) predicted before any DFT existed for them. They come out of a three-step pipeline.

1. **Composition sampling (August).** 4,000 alloy compositions were sampled. 3,339 pass the Hume-Rothery / Ω–δ solid-solution rules, and from those, 12 diverse single-phase candidates were taken forward (`docs/37-hea-screen-result-and-melt-list.md`, `docs/36-screen-validation-and-stability-gate.md`).
2. **The August 5 screen — prediction arm A.**
   - **Method:** for each candidate, the MLIP relaxed the OER intermediates (*OH, *O, *OOH) on 12 surface sites of a rutile-oxide model (4 cus sites × 3 decorations, each from 3 starts). The computational hydrogen electrode turns those energies into an overpotential η, and each alloy is scored at its best site.
   - **Chemical QC:** 6 of the 12 failed it (adsorbates desorbed or broke apart), leaving six clean "gated" alloys.
   - **Stability:** a multi-element Pourbaix gate added a dissolution estimate.
   - **Record:** the result is dated in git (commit 96b1cb1).
3. **The September 13 census — prediction arm B.**
   - **Method:** the same six alloys were re-sampled at 120 sites each (30 random decorations × 4 sites). Each alloy is scored by the 10th percentile of the adsorbate-intact sites, because an alloy's activity is dominated by its best sites and 120 sites expose rare good sites that 12 miss (`docs/95-census-2-3-readout-2026-09-13.md`).
   - **Effect:** this moved Cu8Cr23Mn35Co34 from 5th of the six to 1st: its best site went from 0.756 V among the original 12 to a 10th percentile of 0.384 V over its 16 adsorbate-intact sites (of 120).
   - **Re-rank of record:** the census is also the re-rank the S8 gate requires before any melt (Frank, 2026-10-08: "Let's rerank gate."; freeze proposal §1a).

Batch 1 takes all six gated alloys:
- Five of them, the K-set, were chosen to test the two arms.
- The sixth, Ni34Fe6Cu29Co31, was added on 2026-10-08, so the whole gated set is measured in one batch.

## No batch 2

Earlier versions of this plan said batch 2 "comes from prediction arm C, the DFT stage". That was misleading. DFT never proposes new alloys: arm C only re-scored the six screened alloys, and the only alloy it could have added was Ni34. Two things have since removed batch 2:
- **Ni34 is in batch 1.**
- **Arm C is final (2026-10-08).**
  - It ran fixed-geometry DFT+U single points at 16 census sites: 64 SCFs and one re-run round.
  - It gives values for four alloys (Cu26 0.613 V, Ni31 0.934, Cu22 0.942, Ni34 1.281) and none for Cu8 or Fe25.
  - So its registered tests, including the Ni34 nomination, cannot be evaluated ([arm C design doc](s8-arm-c-dft-design-2026-10-07.md) §7).
  - Its partial order is frozen as an exploratory prediction (freeze proposal §4).

A targeted DFT round for Cu8 and Fe25 (the arm C extension, freeze proposal §4b) may add their values before OER. It is exploratory and does not change the melt set.

A search for new compositions with DFT checks would be a separate experiment with its own freeze, after the Oct 25 data lock (see "Later melts").

## Later melts

None are planned. One would arise in only two situations:

1. **Replicate ingots.** The plan replicates at the coupon level: at least 3 electrodes cut from one button per alloy. That captures electrode-to-electrode variation, but it cannot tell a bad button from a bad alloy. A second, independent button of the decisive alloys (Cu8, Ni31, Fe25) would. If wanted, it is better done inside batch 1 (9 melts) than as a later batch.
2. **A follow-up after the results.** Confirming a winner, or exploring compositions around it, would be a new experiment with its own freeze, after the STS data lock. It is not part of this plan.

## Each melt

1. **Weigh.** Use a fresh weigh sheet for the exact composition ([s8-weigh-sheet-2026-10-08.md](s8-weigh-sheet-2026-10-08.md): about 1,207 g of metal for all six). Each button is 200 g with 4% extra Mn, because Mn evaporates in the arc [CONFIRM the over-charge with FWM at this size].
2. **Arc melt.** Under argon at Fort Wayne Metals (supervised). Flip and remelt 4×.
3. **Anneal.** 1,050 °C for 24 h under argon, then quench.
4. **Check.** SEM-EDS composition within ±2 at.% of target, and XRD phases.
   - A miss is re-melted once.
   - If it misses again, it is kept, flagged and analysed at its measured composition.
   - It is never swapped for another composition.
5. **Electrodes.** At least 3 rotating-disk inserts per alloy plus a spare (5.00 mm × 4.00 mm cylinders, format below; 0.196 cm² face), polished to an identical finish.

## Electrode format (Tackett's reply, 2026-10-07)

Tackett's lab accepts flat coupons. Freeze proposal §6 now specifies the format below, a potted 5.00 mm disk. The 5 × 4 mm Pine-cylinder proposal is superseded. The earlier version is kept in [s8-melt-plan-archive-2026-10-07-pre-tackett-reply.md](s8-melt-plan-archive-2026-10-07-pre-tackett-reply.md).

**His requirements:**
- **Stationary for coupons.** Flat coupons do not fit the rotating-disk hardware, and he expects rotation would not change the outcome. Superseded 2026-10-09 by the 5 mm insert format below, which does fit.
- **Size:** the coupon goes through a 24 mm port, so anything ≤ 20 mm across fits.
- **Mask:** epoxy covers every edge and the whole back, leaving one exposed face of defined area. The face must sit fully under the electrolyte.
- **Back contact:** a wire attached with silver paint, with electrical continuity checked *before* potting. The wire leaves the epoxy at the top of the coupon, above the electrolyte, so it adds no current.
- **Who mounts:** the coupons arrive mounted. Mounting happens before shipping (FWM or PFW).

**Sample: 5 mm rotating-disk insert (Frank, 2026-10-09; pending Tackett's shroud confirmation):**
- **Every sample is a 5.00 mm diameter × 4.00 mm tall cylinder** (Pine ChangeDisk insert format), wire-EDM cut, with one face polished below the EDM recast layer. The exposed area is 0.196 cm².
- **Rotating (1600 rpm proposed), not stationary.** This reverses Tackett's stationary default, which applied to flat coupons. Rotation clears O₂ bubbles during holds.
- **No epoxy or back wire:** the insert press-fits the RDE shroud. Ask Tackett for the shroud type and diameter tolerance.
- **Cutting:** cores are taken through the button height after trimming the bottom and top; about 4–6 per button. Per alloy: ≥ 3 test cylinders, 1 spare, and an offcut for SEM-EDS and XRD.
- **The same cylinder should fit the EC-MS cell** ("standard 5 mm cylindrical electrodes"). Ask whether a 4 mm height works there.
- **Fallback if RDE is refused:** epoxy-mounted 5.00 mm × 1.5 mm stationary disks, potted then polished flush, with a silver-painted back wire.

**O₂ / Faradaic efficiency:**
- He suggests running without O₂ quantification. Most OER papers omit it, and the Tafel slope would show whether something other than OER dominates.
- **Option:** if one alloy can be made into a thin disk of exactly 5.0 mm diameter, he can probably mount it in the Spectro Inlets EC-MS cell to measure O₂ directly. This takes extra time.
- **Proposed EC-MS alloy:** Cu8Cr23Mn35Co34 (K1's pivot; Cr-rich, so Cr oxidation current is the obvious competing reaction), using one of its cylinders.
- **Roadmap §5 requires an O₂ / FE method for an OER activity claim.** The freeze now proposes both routes together [CONFIRM]:
  - the main set without FE, reported as anodic activity checked by the Tafel slope;
  - the optional EC-MS disk, Cu8 first. An OER-specific claim is limited to alloys measured by EC-MS.

**LSV and Tafel (his questions):**

## What the DFT errors changed, and what they did not

- **The endmember reference panel: yes, rankings changed.** These are the DFT calculations on pure rutile oxides that the MLIP was validated against.
  - **The Cr error.** CrO2's *O state was trapped at a long Cr–O bond (2.016 Å). Restarting found a state 1.396 eV lower, moving η(Cr) from 1.726 V to 0.491 V (Aug 3; docs/32, docs/33). Later basin restarts took it to 0.330 V (`tier_v2`, Aug 9; docs/41 §6f). Cr went from behind Mn and Fe to first of the seven endmembers.
  - **Why the OOH repairs did not matter.** The OER score is set by the largest reaction step. Corrections to *OOH did not move Mn or Fe, because their limiting step is *OH → *O (docs/28).
  - **The repairs also changed the MLIP's measured validation error:** 129.6 mV against `tier_v1`, 99.6 mV against `tier_v2`.
- **The alloys: arm C is the first DFT ranking, and it is partial.**
  - Before arm C, no DFT overpotentials existed for the alloys (roadmap §4: "zero ordinary AEM overpotential scores"). So there was no "DFT before the errors" alloy ranking to test.
  - Arm C (final 2026-10-08) gives values for four of the six and none for Cu8 or Fe25.
  - Many of its SCFs stalled in competing magnetic states: the same branch instability seen on the endmembers (arm C design doc §7).
- **The restart issue fixed by P-A (O1, 2026-10-07) does not change rankings.** It perturbs forces by about 1e−4 Ry/bohr and energies by under 0.1 meV; it only blocked certification of the relaxation protocol.
- **Why this matters for the melts.** The corrected endmember panel ranks CrO2 as the most active endmember, ahead of IrO2 and RuO2, which experiment contradicts (docs/36:191). Cu8's census lead rests on Cr sites, so K1 also tests whether that Cr preference holds in a real alloy.
