## Batch 1 — what it is and where it comes from

Batch 1 is the **MLIP set**: five alloys whose activity was predicted by a machine-learned interatomic potential (MACE-MPA-0) before any DFT existed for them. They come out of a three-step pipeline.

1. **Composition sampling (August).** 4,000 alloy compositions were sampled. 3,339 pass the Hume-Rothery / Ω–δ solid-solution rules, and from those, 12 diverse single-phase candidates were taken forward (`docs/37-hea-screen-result-and-melt-list.md`, `docs/36-screen-validation-and-stability-gate.md`).
2. **The August 5 screen — prediction arm A.**
   - **Method:** for each candidate, the MLIP relaxed the OER intermediates (*OH, *O, *OOH) on 12 surface sites of a rutile-oxide model (4 cus sites × 3 decorations, each from 3 starts). The computational hydrogen electrode turns those energies into an overpotential η, and each alloy is scored at its best site.
   - **Chemical QC:** 6 of the 12 failed it (adsorbates desorbed or broke apart), leaving six clean "gated" alloys.
   - **Stability:** a multi-element Pourbaix gate added a dissolution estimate.
   - **Record:** the result is dated in git (commit 96b1cb1).
3. **The September 13 census — prediction arm B.**
   - **Method:** the same six alloys were re-sampled at 120 sites each (30 random decorations × 4 sites). Each alloy is scored by the 10th percentile of the adsorbate-intact sites, because an alloy's activity is dominated by its best sites and 120 sites expose rare good sites that 12 miss (`docs/95-census-2-3-readout-2026-09-13.md`).
   - **Effect:** this moved Cu8Cr23Mn35Co34 from 5th of the six to 1st: its best site went from 0.756 V among the original 12 to a 10th percentile of 0.384 V among the 120.

From those six gated alloys, batch 1 takes the five that test the two arms:

| Alloy | Role | Arm A rank among these five (best of 12) | Arm B rank among these five (p10 of 120) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cu8Cr23Mn35Co34 | Candidate | 4th (0.756 V) | **1st** (0.384 V) |
| Ni31Cr29Cu5Mn35 | Candidate | **1st** (0.440 V) | 2nd (0.473 V) |
| Fe25Co25Ni25Cr25 | Candidate | 2nd (0.453 V) | 4th (0.554 V) |
| Cu26Ni9Cr31Co33 | Candidate | 3rd (0.479 V) | 3rd (0.502 V); 1st under the strict policy |
| Cu22Fe30Co32Mn15 | Anchor | last (0.796 V) | last (0.831 V) |

The sixth gated alloy, Ni34Fe6Cu29Co31, is predicted poor by both arms and is not needed to test them.

**The decisive test is Cu8 against Ni31 and Fe25.** The census says Cu8 is more active than both; the August screen says the opposite. Both arms agree that Cu22 is the least active.

## Batch 2 — where it comes from

Batch 2 comes from **prediction arm C, the DFT stage**. The sequence:

1. O1 (Anvil job 21159532) certifies the stop/resume relaxation protocol (P-A).
2. Production DFT relaxations run on the candidate alloys' sites (clean slab, *OH, *O, *OOH), with reference calculations, giving a DFT η per alloy.
3. Those predictions are frozen and deposited (arm C) before any OER measurement of batch 1 is seen.

If DFT simply re-orders the five batch-1 alloys, **there is no batch 2**: everything it ranks is already melted. Batch 2 is needed only if DFT nominates an alloy that is not in batch 1. Within the census, that can only be Ni34Fe6Cu29Co31.

Batch 2 would contain:
- the nominated alloy;
- a re-melt of the anchor Cu22;
- if feasible, a re-melt of one batch-1 alloy.

The re-melts are bridges. Ingots melted on different days, from different feedstock, in a different furnace run, can differ slightly. Measuring the same alloy in both batches shows how large that batch effect is, so it is not mistaken for a difference between alloys.

Timing is tight. DFT production after O1, then the arm-C deposit, then a melt, a 24 h anneal, checks and OER all have to fit before the Oct 25 data-lock checkpoint. If batch 2 cannot be measured in time, its alloy is reported as an untested prediction. The proposal's [CONFIRM] option removes this risk: melt Ni34Fe6Cu29Co31 as a sixth batch-1 alloy now. It contains no Cr.

## Is there a batch 3?

No. One would arise in only two situations:

1. **Replicate ingots.** The plan replicates at the coupon level: at least 3 electrodes cut from one ingot per alloy. That captures electrode-to-electrode variation, but it cannot tell a bad ingot from a bad alloy. A second, independent ingot of the decisive alloys (Cu8, Ni31, Fe25) would. If wanted, it is better done inside batch 1 (8 melts) than as a later batch.
2. **A follow-up after the results.** Confirming a winner, or exploring compositions around it, would be a new experiment with its own freeze, after the STS data lock. It is not part of this plan.

## Each melt

1. **Weigh.** A fresh weigh sheet for the exact composition ([s8-weigh-sheet-2026-10-07.md](s8-weigh-sheet-2026-10-07.md): about 50.3 g of metal for all five); 10 g ingot with 4% extra Mn (Mn evaporates in the arc).
2. **Arc melt.** Under argon at Fort Wayne Metals (supervised). Flip and remelt 4×.
3. **Anneal.** 1,050 °C for 24 h under argon, then quench.
4. **Check.** SEM-EDS composition within ±2 at.% of target, and XRD phases.
   - A miss is re-melted once.
   - If it misses again, it is kept, flagged and analysed at its measured composition.
   - It is never swapped for another composition.
5. **Electrodes.** At least 3 coupons per alloy, mounted, polished and epoxy-masked to a defined area.

## Order of operations

1. Cr(VI) risk assessment, mentor-signed. **Blocking:** four of the five alloys contain 22.6–31.5 at.% Cr.
2. Frank confirms the [CONFIRM] items; the freeze is deposited.
3. Fresh weigh sheets.
4. Batch 1 melt and anneal; composition and phase checks.
5. Electrode preparation; electrochemistry dry runs on IrO2 and blanks only.
6. No OER on the five alloys until the DFT predictions (arm C) are deposited, or Oct 21, whichever comes first.
7. OER measurements: at least 3 coupons per alloy over at least 2 bench days, with IrO2 and a blank each day.
8. Score K1 (Cu8 vs Ni31 and Fe25) and K2 (Cu22 last) against all three arms.

## What the DFT errors changed, and what they did not

- **The endmember reference panel: yes, rankings changed.** These are the DFT calculations on pure rutile oxides that the MLIP was validated against.
  - **The Cr error.** CrO2's *O state was trapped at a long Cr–O bond (2.016 Å). Restarting found a state 1.396 eV lower, moving η(Cr) from 1.726 V to 0.491 V (Aug 3; docs/32, docs/33). Later basin restarts took it to 0.330 V (`tier_v2`, Aug 9; docs/41 §6f). Cr went from behind Mn and Fe to first of the seven endmembers.
  - **Why the OOH repairs did not matter.** The OER score is set by the largest reaction step. Corrections to *OOH did not move Mn or Fe, because their limiting step is *OH → *O (docs/28).
  - **The repairs also changed the MLIP's measured validation error:** 129.6 mV against `tier_v1`, 99.6 mV against `tier_v2`.
- **The alloys: no change yet, because there is nothing to change.** No DFT overpotentials exist for the alloys (roadmap §4: "zero ordinary AEM overpotential scores"). The endmember repairs do not re-rank them by themselves, so there is no "DFT before the errors" alloy ranking to test. Arm C will be the first DFT ranking of these alloys.
- **The restart issue now being fixed (P-A, O1) does not change rankings.** It perturbs forces by about 1e−4 Ry/bohr and energies by under 0.1 meV; it only blocked certification of the relaxation protocol.
- **Why this matters for the melts.** The corrected endmember panel ranks CrO2 as the most active endmember, ahead of IrO2 and RuO2, which experiment contradicts (docs/36:191). Cu8's census lead rests on Cr sites, so K1 also tests whether that Cr preference holds in a real alloy.
