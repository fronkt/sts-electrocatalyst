Methods 
 
We subtracted the data points (∆E) from a total collection of 24 papers. We converted Gibbs free energy 
(∆G) values into the ∆E when it was needed. Because we used ∆E values on the graphs related to scaling 
relation. ∆E is calculated  by the following expression, ∆E= ∆G -∆ZPE+T∆S, where ∆ZP E is the 
difference in Zero Point  Energy due to the reaction and ∆S the change in entropy. In the case that the 
∆ZPE and ∆S were not provided, the corrections from Rossmeisl et al. [25] were applied to the data in 
order to obtain ∆E values. Furthermore, to the construction of the volcano plot in Fig.3, we used the ΔG 
values calculated by the following expression ∆G= ∆E+∆ZPE-T∆S, where we used the same ΔZPE and 
ΔS from Table SI-1 for all the data.

Fig SI-2: A screening using transition metals from the d-block as dopants in the TiO2 structure was made 
to draw graph corresponding to a scaling relation as seen on Figure 8 in the main text.  Both n-type and 
p-type were used in a total number of 23 dopants (Cr, Mn, Cu, Ru, Rh, Pd, Pt, Os, Ir, Au,V , Nb, Mo, W, 
Fe, Co, Ni, Zn, Cd, Ag, Re, Z r, Hf). As it was expected using only one dopant in the unit cell creates a 
trend line for O*, with a slope very close to 1, on the contrary using two dopants the slope is close to 2. 
This is happening due to the change of the binding energy of the O * for the case of the n -type dopants 
when two dopants are used. On the other hand, using one or two the p-type dopants in the unit cell does 
not affect the binding energy of the O * since even one p -type dopant is providing a hole to the system 
and places the Fermi level in the valence band. The density of states for all the tested dopants are provided 
in the following diagrams. Chosen dopants have been highli ghted on periodic table (blue square frame 
represents host oxide (TiO2); red square frame represents n-type dopants; green square frame represents 
rest of the dopants (oxidation state of dopant equal to the host (+4) or less than host (+4>).
